01

Where to Start

When you are not sure what comes next, begin here. We will take the decisions one at a time.

When you first begin designing a funeral, the choices can feel overwhelming. The process comes down to two parts: how you would like to honor your loved one's life, and how you would like to care for their physical remains.

Designing a Service

A funeral gives family and friends a place to gather, support one another, and begin healing. It can be formal or informal, traditional or entirely your own. Services can be held at our funeral home, in a church, at home, or outdoors, and can include music, poetry, or art.

Our staff is experienced in accommodating all beliefs, faiths, lifestyles, and relationships. We will answer your questions, explain the decisions, and encourage you to take the time you need.

Caring for a Loved One's Physical Remains

Once you have chosen how to celebrate your loved one's life, the next decision is how to care for their remains. We will explain the options and help you make the choice that is right for your family.

  • For burial, you will choose a casket and a cemetery, and decide between ground burial and entombment in a mausoleum.
  • For cremation, you will choose an urn and decide whether to bury, entomb, or scatter the cremated remains.

No matter which path you choose, we will stay with you through each decision.

02

Funeral Services

A ceremony gives grief a place to go. We will help you shape one that feels true to your loved one.

Coping with a loss is never easy, and a service is an essential part of the recovery process. It gives those who are grieving a supportive environment in which to find closure, say goodbye, and come to terms with the loss.

Gathering with friends and family gives everyone the opportunity to connect, share memories, offer sympathy, and build a lasting network of comfort. Whatever the setting, style, or direction you choose, our staff will help you create a service that reflects your loved one's life.

Burial Services

Immediate Burial
Burial or entombment without a public service or gathering.
Visitation
Also called a viewing, wake, or calling hours. Family and friends gather with your loved one, in an open or closed casket, to say goodbye and offer support.
Funeral or Memorial Services
A ceremony at our funeral home, a church, or your home that celebrates, honors, and remembers a life. Visitation and the service can both be personalized.
Graveside, Chapel, or Committal Services
Held at the cemetery, so family and friends can be present as your loved one is committed to their final resting place.

Cremation Services

One of the most common misconceptions about cremation is that a funeral or visitation is not possible. It is. You may hold a final viewing before cremation, or a formal service before or after. There are also several choices for final disposition:

Interment
Burial or entombment of cremated remains in a family plot, memorial site, cremation niche, urn garden, or another meaningful location.
Graveside Services
A service at the burial of cremated remains, with memorial prayers or other meaningful tributes.
Scattering
Cremated remains spread in a memorial garden, over water, or at another meaningful site. Some families scatter a portion and keep the rest in an urn.
Burial at Sea
Cremated remains released into the ocean, typically from a ship or boat.
Multiple Urns
Cremated remains divided among family members so that each household can keep a loved one nearby.

Ask our staff for a detailed list of interment possibilities in our area.

03

Celebrating Life

Personalization helps family and friends feel connected to the life being honored.

Personalization is not only about honoring your loved one's individuality. It helps family and friends feel connected to the service and the life being honored. A memorable, meaningful service can be an important part of healing.

Honor What Made Them Unique

We will get to know your loved one's story and find ways to represent the bond you shared. A service can reflect hobbies, faith, culture, music, food, and the small details that made someone themselves.

Personalized Printing

From the register book and memorial folders to prayer cards and thank-you notes, our stationery is available in more than 500 themes. We will help you choose a design that honors your loved one's style and creates keepsakes for family and guests.

Tribute Wall

Our online tribute pages let family and friends share stories, photographs, and video, leave condolences, and revisit memories whenever they need to. Guests can view the obituary and service information, and order gifts and flowers directly through the page.

04

Honoring Veterans

We are proud to help families coordinate military tributes and understand the benefits available to them.

We are proud to do our part in honoring our country's veterans. Our duty is to provide professional, dignified assistance and to support you in honoring your loved one's dedication and service.

Traditional Elements & Honors

Standard honor military funerals can include:

  • A military chaplain for family members.
  • A flag presentation service by the chaplain of the American Legion.
  • A final salute at the gravesite.

Full honor military funerals include all standard honors, plus:

  • A 16-gun salute for general officers and flag officers, depending on rank.
  • The U.S. flag draped over the casket and presented to the next of kin.
  • The playing of Taps by a lone bugler or an audio recording.

Other Veteran Benefits

We can help you communicate with the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs to arrange:

  • A Presidential Memorial Certificate, issued in the name of an honorably discharged, deceased veteran.
  • Headstones or markers for eligible veterans and dependents.
  • Memorial plots in a national cemetery when remains are not available for burial.
  • Burial in a VA national cemetery, including the gravesite, opening and closing, grave box, and perpetual care.
  • Reimbursement of eligible burial expenses.

Additional resources: U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs, Veterans' Funeral and Burial Benefits, and Honoring Those Who Served.

05

Final Interment

A permanent place to visit can give family and friends a focus for memory and reflection.

Ground Burial

For a traditional in-ground burial, your family can choose among estate lots, multiple-space lots, or single graves at local cemeteries. We will walk you through the burial grounds in our community and help you find an option that fits your needs, budget, and preferences.

A Lasting Tribute

The grieving process does not end when the funeral does. A permanent place to visit can give family and friends a focus for memory and a private space for reflection. It can also make meaningful dates, such as birthdays and anniversaries, a little easier to face.

If you would like help choosing a cemetery or memorial, call us at (718) 712-1900. We will explain the options without pressure.

Pre-planning

Put your wishes in writing, at your own pace.

Pre-planning gives you control over the decisions that matter, and gives your family room to focus on healing.

Start the form Call (718) 712-1900

Why plan ahead

A decision made now is a burden lifted later.

When a loved one passes, a family faces an overwhelming number of tasks and decisions while struggling through grief. By taking care of your arrangements before the time of need, you relieve them of that burden and give them more time to focus on remembrance.

Planning can be meaningful for your family, too, if you choose to include them in the process.
From our pre-planning guide
It lets you decide how you'd like to be remembered.
Creating your own life celebration ensures that your wishes are respected. However you decide to be memorialized, pre-planning gives you control over the personal decisions that must be made.
It takes the burden off family and friends.
Your family will not have to ask themselves, “Am I making the right choices?” They can focus on saying goodbye and celebrating your life with the people who cared about you most.
It gives you the ability to pre-pay, if you wish.
Pre-funding is not required in order to pre-plan, but pre-payment sets funds aside and protects them against inflation. Our pre-plan funds are placed with Cooperative Funeral Fund, Inc. We would be glad to explain how pre-funding works.
What if I move?
Your plan is stored in our files as a record of your wishes and is easily transferable to any funeral home in the United States. It can follow you wherever you relocate.
Where do I start?
Review the checklist below to gather the information you will need. Then use the pre-planning form to begin, or call us to schedule a time to meet with a member of our staff.

Pre-planning checklist

Information to gather, decisions to consider.

Vital statistics
  • Your father's name
  • Your mother's maiden name
  • Your level of education
  • Your Social Security number
Affiliations
  • Religious, civic, and community memberships
  • Military service and veteran status
  • Organizations that may wish to participate
  • Charities you would like donations directed to
General
  • Gather personal information for your obituary
  • Choose a charity to direct donations to, if desired
  • Choose a funeral home
  • Note any special instructions for your services and final disposition
Funeral service
  • Choose burial or cremation
  • Select a casket or cremation container
  • Choose the location and type of service
  • Decide whether you would like a viewing or visitation
  • Choose a floral arrangement
  • Select photographs and memorabilia to display
  • Consider customs, traditions, or religious rites
  • Choose the clothing you would like to be dressed in
  • Select music and hymns, if desired
  • Select stationery and a memorial register book
  • Arrange transportation for your remains and your family
  • Brainstorm ways to personalize the service
Participants
  • Choose a clergy member or officiator
  • Select musicians
  • Select pallbearers
  • Choose family or friends for the eulogy, scripture, or a speech
Cemetery
  • Choose a cemetery
  • Select a burial or cremation plot, or a mausoleum
  • Decide between above- and below-ground burial
  • Select a memorial or grave marker and inscription

There is no need to make every decision right away. The purpose of pre-planning is to give yourself the time you need to consider your options and make the choices that are right for you.
